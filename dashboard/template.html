  • Under the mandate the four estimators built from daily returns realise {{te_lo}} to {{te_hi}} basis points a year of tracking error and the monthly sample covariance {{te_monthly}}, so the choice of estimator still matters. The factor and principal-component models realise 7 to 9 basis points more than the sample covariance and shrinkage, each difference statistically significant at the 5 per cent level on a paired test after Holm's correction across nine comparisons. The sample covariance and shrinkage differ by 0.2 basis points, which is not statistically significant, and the data rule out a difference of more than about half a basis point. The monthly covariance's 11 basis points more is not statistically significant either, and with {{months}} months the test has too little power to detect a difference of that size.
  • Every estimator's forecast underestimates the realised tracking error: the realised active return is {{bias_pct_lo}} to {{bias_pct_hi}} per cent more volatile than forecast. For the daily estimators the underestimate comes from the years before 2000, and from 2000 their bias statistics are {{split_daily_from_lo}} to {{split_daily_from_hi}}, none statistically significantly different from 1 at the 5 per cent level (Figure {{n:bias}}). Scaling each forecast by the same path's previous 60 months brings the daily estimators to {{cal_lo}} to {{cal_hi}}. The rules-based portfolio reports the bias of its own risk forecast by the same measure.
  • The tilt alone is associated with an active return of {{tilt_lo}} to {{tilt_hi}} basis points a year against a standard error of {{tilt_se_lo}} to {{tilt_se_hi}}, which is not statistically significant at the 5 per cent level, and the data rule out a cost of more than about 25 basis points a year. Under the full mandate the active return is {{gross_lo}} to {{gross_hi}} basis points a year before costs, not statistically significant at that level on any path.
  • The replication reproduces {{gated_pass}} of the {{gated_n}} Sharpe ratios of DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal (2009) that carry a band of 0.03, 26 of 27 counting the value-weighted market once, and all {{turn_n}} turnover figures within 25 per cent. Whether the paper's Sharpe ratios and turnover figures reproduce, and how the rules have done since.

DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal (2009) asked whether optimised portfolios beat 1/N out of sample and found that none of their optimising rules did so reliably. Their Tables 3 and 5 are rebuilt here on four of the five datasets the paper takes from French's library, against bands fixed before the code ran, and the comparison is run forward to {{last_w}}.

The exposure to four factors obtained by spending a budget of forecast tracking error, and the tracking error and return that followed.

The same mandate, with the question the other way round. Each month the optimiser spends a budget of forecast tracking error, {{fo_budgets}} basis points a year, on the factor objective, i.e., the sum of the portfolio's active exposures to value, profitability, investment and momentum, each counted in the direction of its premium and weighted equally, under every rule of the mandate. Nineteen paths run over the {{months}} months from {{first_w}} to {{last_w}}, fifteen main paths (the five covariance estimators at the three budgets) and four more at 100 basis points (the robust portfolio, RiskMetrics, and the sample covariance targeting value alone and momentum alone). Seven expectations were written before any path ran, and Table {{n:verdicts}} states each with its verdict: {{fo_met_w}} are met and {{fo_not_met_w}} are not. The targeted factors' contributions are their realised returns over these months times the exposures obtained, and the study estimates no premium.

The data, what is known at each date, the costs, what the study cannot show, and the terms.

Method

Universe and period. Parts 2 and 3 run on Kenneth R. French's 49 value-weighted industry portfolios and his six factors, downloaded at run time, vintage {{vintage}}. The benchmark is the cap-weighted combination of the 49, rebuilt from French's firm counts and average sizes. Every path runs over the {{months}} months from {{first_w}} to {{last_w}}. The replication uses four of the five datasets the paper takes from the library (Industry, MKT/SMB/HML, FF-1-factor, FF-4-factor), 1963 to {{paper_hi_w}} with a 120-month estimation window, so {{paper_lo_w}} to {{paper_hi_w}} out of sample, and runs the same rules forward to {{last_w}}.

Rebalancing. Monthly. The optimiser forms each month's weights from the previous month's weights after the month's returns have moved them, under the constraint set in force. The turnover cap of 2 per cent one-way a month is relaxed to the smallest feasible turnover in the months in which the benchmark itself moves more than the cap allows, and those months are counted.

What is known at each date. The covariance comes from the window before the month (120 monthly returns, or three years of daily returns), the industry betas from the previous three years of daily returns, and the calibration of a forecast from the same path's previous 60 months. Nothing from the month itself or later enters a weight. French's files are a single vintage, so every month's returns are as French had revised them at the download date, which a portfolio run in real time would not have had.

Costs. Returns are gross of fees. Trading cost is charged at 50 basis points per unit of one-way turnover where stated (notebook 12 also charges 100). The benchmark itself turns over {{bench_turn}} per cent a month, which costs an index fund {{bench_cost}} basis points a year at the same rate.

Factor exposure. The factor objective's portfolio maximises the sum of its active exposures to the four targeted factors, each in the direction of its premium and weighted equally in beta units, under every rule of the mandate and a budget on its forecast tracking error. The betas are those of the three years of daily returns before the month, and the forecast comes from the same covariance estimate as under the mandate. A month in which the mandate's tracking-error-minimising portfolio already exceeds the budget takes that portfolio and is counted. Every expectation was written before any path ran, and the verdicts not met are reported in the same words as those met, each with its arithmetic.

What the study cannot show. It is a simulation on industry portfolios, so a stock-level mandate may behave differently. No path's active return is statistically significant at the 5 per cent level, in part 2 or in part 3. The data rule out a cost of the tilt in return of more than about 25 basis points a year, the factor objective's return at 100 basis points would need about 34 to 43 basis points a year to be statistically significant, and no claim about skill or a premium follows. The forecasts are those of five covariance estimators on one data set, and the tracking errors realised here are what those estimators and this mandate produced over these 47 years. The write-up is the README and the two notes in the repository, where every number comes from the tables under results/{{vintage}}/.

Terms

1/N
the rule that holds every asset at the same weight and rebalances to it each month.
Rule
any of the nine portfolio rules of the replication: 1/N, sample-based mean-variance, Bayes-Stein, minimum variance, the value-weighted market, the short-sale-constrained versions of the three optimising rules, and the generalised minimum-variance rule.
Bayes-Stein
the rule of Jorion (1986) that shrinks each estimated mean towards one common value.
Estimation window
the months of returns a rule sees when it forms its weights, 120 in the paper.
Out-of-sample
the months after a rule's estimation window, on which its weights are tested.
Sharpe ratio
the mean monthly excess return, i.e., the return minus the one-month Treasury bill rate, divided by its standard deviation.
Turnover
the sum of absolute weight changes at a rebalance. One-way turnover is half of it.
Turnover cap
the limit on one-way turnover, 2 per cent a month under the mandate.
Band
an interval, fixed before the code ran, within which a replicated number counts as matching the published one. A cell with a band receives a verdict, pass or miss.
Vintage
the version of French's files, named by the CRSP database they were built from.
Jobson-Korkie test
the test of whether two Sharpe ratios differ, with Memmel's correction, as the paper uses it.
Cap-weighted
weighted by market capitalisation, i.e., each company's share price times its shares outstanding.
Value-weighted
the same as cap-weighted, French's word for his portfolios.
Benchmark
the index the portfolio tracks, the cap-weighted combination of the 49 industries.
Tracking error
the standard deviation of the monthly difference between the portfolio's return and the benchmark's, times the square root of 12. The forecast is the optimiser's own, and the realised value is the measured one.
Active weight
an industry's weight in the portfolio minus its benchmark weight.
Active return
the portfolio's return minus the benchmark's.
Basis point
a hundredth of a percentage point.
Covariance estimator
a method that turns a window of returns into a covariance matrix: the sample covariance, Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage towards a scaled identity, a six-factor model and a principal-component model. The robust portfolio minimises the largest forecast across the five estimates, and RiskMetrics is an exponentially weighted sample covariance.
Shrinkage
Ledoit and Wolf's estimate, a weighted average of the sample covariance and a scaled identity matrix, with the weight chosen from the data.
Beta
the slope of a return regressed on the benchmark's return or on a factor's.
Minimum-variance portfolio
the portfolio with the smallest forecast variance under its constraints.
Tilt
holding Coal, Oil and Utilities at no more than half their benchmark weight.
Mandate
the rules the portfolio obeys in the second and third parts: long-only, each industry outside the tilt within 2 percentage points of its benchmark weight, one-way turnover of at most 2 per cent a month, the tilt, tobacco and weapons excluded, and a beta of one to the benchmark.
Constraint set
one of the cumulative sets C0 to C3 of Figure {{n:frontier}}: long-only, then the active weight bound, then the turnover cap, then the mandate. The tilt alone is the turnover-capped set with the tilt.
Path
one covariance estimator run under one constraint set month by month over the {{months}} months, or, in the third part, one estimator at one budget.
Bias statistic
the standard deviation of the monthly active return divided by the same month's forecast. It is 1 for an accurate forecast and above 1 when the forecast is too low.
Block bootstrap
resampling the months in blocks of twelve, so that the months of a block keep their order, and recomputing a statistic on each of 2,000 resamples. The band is the interval between the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles.
Factor
a return series that many assets move with: the market (Mkt-RF), size (SMB), value (HML), profitability (RMW), investment (CMA) and momentum (Mom) factors of Fama and French and Carhart.
Active exposure
the sum over industries of the active weight times the industry's beta to the factor, i.e., the slope of its return on the factor's.
Attribution
the split of a month's active return into six contributions, each the active exposure to a factor times the factor's return, and a residual.
Residual
the part of the active return the six factors do not explain, i.e., the active return minus the six contributions.
Factor share
the share of the variance of the active return that the six contributions explain.
Standard error
the standard deviation of an estimate. For a mean active return over 47 years, it is the realised tracking error over the square root of 47.
Factor objective
the sum of the portfolio's active exposures to the targeted factors, which the optimiser maximises in the third part.
Targeted factors
value, profitability, investment and momentum, each counted in the direction of its premium and weighted equally in beta units. The study estimates no premium.
Budget
the limit on the forecast tracking error of the factor objective's portfolio, 75, 100 or 150 basis points a year.
Main path
one of the fifteen paths of the five covariance estimators at the three budgets.
Targeted total
the sum of the four targeted factors' contributions to a path's active return, per year.
Exposure per basis point
a path's mean active exposure divided by its realised tracking error in basis points a year.
Gap
the factor objective chosen with the betas estimated before a month minus the value the same weights carry with the betas estimated after it. The gap per unit divides a path's mean absolute gap by its mean factor objective.
